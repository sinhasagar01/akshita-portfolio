The Details contract · the switch row, and the canvas

Selecting Details gives an empty canvas beside a full inspector. Its five fields — title, hero, summary, type, platform — are exactly what one public element renders: the project card in the work section. So the canvas is not being given something to display; it is being given the thing those fields are for. Both hover states are drawn, because the summary only exists on hover and the author is editing that text while they look at it.

Each label sits above its switch rather than beside it, both columns are flex-direction: column with the label align-self: flex-start, and the shared parent is justify-content: space-between. That is the desired-design reference, built.

Why both hover states are drawn

The summary is invisible at rest. The card shows its image, title and platform label; the summary only appears in the hover overlay. A canvas that draws one state cannot show a field the inspector spends a whole textarea on — and the author is editing that text while looking at it. Same shape as #198's finding: a defect that lives in the comparison rather than in either state, visible only by driving both.

Two fields that do not render, stated rather than papered over

Category is not on the card — it decides which filter tab the card appears under, so its preview is the filter row, drawn above with the matching tab outlined. Type renders nowhere on any surface this canvas can draw, and the honest thing is to say so rather than invent a surface for it.

⚠ Fidelity, and a hazard that will fire here

The canvas renders ProjectCard itself, with values from the draft form rather than from disk — #178's rule for the blog canvas, applied. A hand-built lookalike would drift the first time the card changes, and it would drift silently. Hazard 21 will fire on this surface. HERO_IMAGE_UNSUITABLE still lists elevate-one-view, so that card renders the hand-built mock rather than its uploaded hero — and its comment describes a 390×988 portrait that no longer exists. On the canvas that will look like the upload did not work. Whoever builds this meets the hazard, and it is a content decision rather than a lookup.

Inline editing is not in the first pass

inlineEditProps exists and the mechanism is proven, but the card's title sits inside a block-level <Link> — a locked decision from #160 — and making text inside a link editable is a real interaction problem rather than an attribute. The canvas tracks the inspector live and the inspector stays the one place these fields are written, which is the posture #190 chose for the blog head for a comparable reason.
