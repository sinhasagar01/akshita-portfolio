#168 recorded the shadow literal as an EXCEPTION — StudioModal authors one because the theme has no elevation scale, and that was written down as an exception rather than a convention. Three steps across fourteen cards is not an exception; it is a scale. If D wins it gets declared beside the radius and motion blocks with its steps named by role, or the next surface picks a shadow by copy-paste. The elevation scale — declared, because three steps is a scale

#168 recorded the shadow literal as an exception, not a convention — StudioModal authors one because the theme has no elevation scale. Three steps across fourteen cards is not an exception; it is a scale arriving undeclared. So it is declared beside the radius and motion blocks, scoped to .studio-chrome, named by role exactly as those are.
tokenrole
--studio-lift-resta card sitting on the surface
--studio-lift-hovera card the pointer is over, plus a 2px rise
--studio-lift-activethe selected card
A new elevated surface picks by role, never by copying a literal — the whole reason radius and ground are named that way. If only one step is ever used, do not declare three: a scale with one consumer is the FIT_THRESHOLD_PX shape this project has deleted three times.

⚠ Two things to measure rather than assume

A shadow costs layout nothing and paint a lot. Fourteen blurred shadows, repainting on hover, on a surface that also holds a scaled canvas. This is the first time the studio paints elevation at grid scale. And the ground moves. Cards are cream-50 on a cream-100 grid, because a cream-50 card on a cream-50 page has nothing to lift off. That is not incidental — it is what makes the elevation legible, and it checks out against the ladder: the grid is a field surface holding cards, which is what cream-100 is for.

The left edge

The card keeps a 3px left edge whose colour is the only thing that moves — transparent at rest and hover, accent-500 when selected — so nothing reflows between states and selection cannot shift the grid by a pixel. There is no border shorthand on this element at all — border:0 plus one left declaration — which sidesteps hazard 26 by construction rather than by care.

Reorder is ← / →

Document order runs left-to-right and wraps, so the section before "Goals" is visually to its left. Today's ↑/↓ inherit a single-column model the layout no longer matches — ↑ on the first card of a row moves it to the end of the previous row, a jump rather than a step. The first ← and the last → are disabled rather than absent, so the control does not move between cards.

The default view

A case study opens on Editor, toggle reading Editor | Board. Today it opens on the Board — showing the shape when what an author came to do is write.

The shape, and its honest risk

One mini per block kind; a new kind needs a new mini or falls back to the generic bars. The minis are abstractions, not renders — a mini that stops matching what the section actually draws is worse than no mini, because it teaches the wrong thing. Whoever adds a block kind adds its mini in the same PR.
