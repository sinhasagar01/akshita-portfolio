⚠ Two defects #237 shipped and caught, and both apply again

An in-flow handle consumes layout width. #237's first version took 4px off the work area — a term nobody put in the arithmetic, introduced by the control built to sit on the seam. At a 288px sidebar the canvas measured 645 where every sum promised 649. The grip is absolute on both seams and consumes nothing. Half the hit area was dead. -mr-1 let main paint over it, so a pointerdown at the seam reported a button in main as its target and the drag never began — and it looked fine. Prove the 12px target with a pointerdown returning the handle, not by looking at it.

⚠ Ink and cream are one rule, not two designs

One step of separation from the surface the grip sits on, expressed in two directions. A single colour for both is what makes a control look broken on the sidebar. And the ink seam's contrast must be measured on ink, not carried over. #214 moved the topbar to solid ink and six of seven on-ink ratios changed while one fell below its floor — "the value did not change; its ground did."

Why the grip won

A pane at zero width has no handle inside it. The grip rides the edge, so the affordance and its way back are the same object in the same place — no separate rail, nothing that exists only in one state. And it is the only treatment that announces itself at rest: an author learns the panes resize without having to brush the seam to find out. ⚠ The way back must be keyboard-reachable. A drag-only affordance is a pane a keyboard user can close and never reopen. #237's keys are the precedent — arrows step, Home/End jump, and Enter toggles minimum against the last non-minimum, because binding it to a fixed default hands a keyboard user the one width they already had.
