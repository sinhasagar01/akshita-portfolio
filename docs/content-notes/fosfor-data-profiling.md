## Project overview (About data profiling)

Data profiling is the process of systematically analyzing data sets to evaluate their quality, structure, and content. It identifies anomalies, missing values, and patterns, ensuring data accuracy and consistency.

Organizations use data profiling to enhance decision-making and improve business analytics. It also facilitates data standardization and compliance with industry regulations.

By automating data profiling, companies can efficiently handle large datasets and maintain data quality across systems.

**Image:** `/images/data-profiling-overview.png` — *What data profiling covers.*

## Requirement

Build the capability to compute statistical metrics on any given data model. Data profiling is a fundamental step in understanding the structure and consistency of data — it lets designers and analysts make informed decisions about data cleansing, transformation, or validation. This spans **basic profiling** (examining basic statistical measures of data) and **advanced profiling** (identifying value distribution across columns, patterns, anomalies, outliers, and potential data quality issues).

Fosfor, a collaborative data workspace, offers a range of tools to manage data pipelines, projects, and workflows. One of the key challenges users face is data profiling — understanding the quality, distribution, and characteristics of their data to ensure its accuracy and relevance. This case study explores how I led the design process for the data profiling feature in Fosfor, aiming to give users intuitive, interactive tools for inspecting and understanding their data sets.

## The problem

Fosfor's users — data analysts, data engineers, and business intelligence professionals — struggled to understand the underlying structure and quality of their data before using it in analysis or reporting. Without clear, actionable insights, they spent considerable time manually inspecting data, which slowed down decision-making and increased the potential for errors.

## The challenge

In a data-driven world, businesses face the daunting task of extracting meaningful insights from vast, complex datasets. Traditional analysis methods often fall short, leading to:

- **Data silos** — Data scattered across multiple sources (databases, spreadsheets, APIs) prevents a unified view of customer behavior.
  `/images/challenge-data-silos.png`
- **Data quality issues** — Inaccurate, incomplete, or inconsistent data leads to erroneous conclusions and ineffective decisions.
  `/images/challenge-data-quality.png`
- **Time-consuming analysis** — Manual data cleaning, transformation, and analysis are slow and error-prone.
  `/images/challenge-time-consuming.png`
- **Lack of actionable insights** — Raw data alone has limited value; businesses need actionable insight to improve experiences, optimize operations, and drive growth.
  `/images/challenge-actionable-insights.png`

## Process

1. **Research & analysis** — Conducted competitor analysis and online research to gather insights.
2. **Information architecture** — Restructured navigation and content based on findings, prioritizing features by user need.
3. **Wireframing & prototyping** — Designed low-fidelity wireframes to visualize the new layout, iterated on feedback, then built a high-fidelity interactive prototype for testing.
4. **Usability testing** — Ran tests with a diverse group of users to validate the design and surface improvements, then adjusted accordingly.
5. **Visual design & style guide** — Developed a cohesive visual language (color, typography, iconography) and a style guide to keep future updates consistent.

## Success metrics

Metrics used to evaluate the effectiveness and impact of the data profiling feature:

### Reduction in time spent on data quality checks
- **Metric:** The reduction in time users spend manually inspecting data for quality issues, before vs. after the feature.
- **Why it matters:** Less time spent shows the tool effectively automates and streamlines profiling, improving overall productivity.

### Data issue detection rate
- **Metric:** The number of comments, annotations, and collaborations occurring around data quality issues in the profiling tool.
- **Why it matters:** Indicates how effectively teams work together to resolve data quality problems.

### Improvement in data quality metrics
- **Metric:** A measurable improvement in key data quality metrics (completeness, consistency, accuracy) post-implementation.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates the feature has a direct impact on maintaining clean, usable data.

### Frequency of use
- **Metric:** How often users interact with the feature (daily, weekly, monthly).
- **Why it matters:** Frequent use shows users rely on it as a crucial part of their workflow.
