Ash — the snap, reversed

Both motions at once. Assemble runs on load, on Replay and on every tab change — the embers stream in and the interface pieces spring into the panel's edge. Magnetic is always live underneath it: move your pointer across the artwork and the figure, the ember field and each piece separate by depth.

⚠ The two motions cannot share one transform, and that is the thing to get right. A CSS animation with forwards beats a plain transform, so a piece that both enters and follows the pointer would freeze at its entrance value. Every moving thing here is two elements — an outer box carrying the pointer transform and an inner one carrying the entrance. That is why the artwork sits inside a .figure wrapper and each piece inside a .pin.

⚠ Neither motion hides the illustration, which is what the source mockup got wrong. Its Assemble opened with clip-path: inset(0 100% 0 0) plus blur(5px) and saturate(.72) — for the first fraction of a second the artwork was not on screen. Here the entrance settles a 1.2% scale and nothing else, and the pointer only translates and tilts. No clip-path, no mask, no filter, no opacity change in either.

⚠ Nothing is applied to the illustration — no filter, no mask, no blend, no opacity change. It renders exactly as drawn on every palette. The embers are synthetic panels that assemble behind her at z-index 4 against her 6, so she is never covered and never altered. The cost of that is stated rather than hidden: the artwork draws its own cards and blocks, so on the saturated palettes there are two families of shape in the frame at once.

Palette

Thousands of embers stream in from the edges and settle into themed panels, a block and a disc. On a tab change they let go, drift, and re-gather into a different arrangement. Closest to the reference: matter dispersing and returning.

⚠ Three fixes carried over from reading the source mockup. Its orb and dot grid sat at z-index 2 and 1 under an opaque raster and could never be seen — two of six layers animated and rendered nothing. Its synthetic cards landed on cards the artwork already draws, so Magnetic parallaxed them apart and showed the twin. And its pieces waited 0.62s for a reveal to finish. Here the pieces sit at z-index 8 above the figure, in the panel's own empty edge rather than on top of drawn shapes, and start at 0.16s.

Depth comes from divergent multipliers, not from stacking order — the figure moves ×7, the ember canvas ×14 the other way, the cursor ×18, the spark ×20, the blue card ×24, the lime card ×34. That is the source mockup's own idea and it is the part worth keeping.

Roughly 1,600 particles on a canvas at device pixel ratio, and the loop stops when the last one lands.
